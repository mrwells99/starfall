# Outlaw Defense Detonation aiming

Settings now offers **General sensitivity** and **Aiming sensitivity (ADS)** sliders, each from 0.25x to 3.00x in 0.01 steps. Apply saves both. The ADS value independently controls mouse look while an aiming ability owns input, including entry into Defense Detonation; normal look resumes immediately on exit. Existing saved general sensitivity supplies the initial ADS value to preserve the old feel. Neither slider changes keyboard turning, recoil, camera zoom, or movement speed. Future aiming abilities can use `player_options.mouse_sensitivity(true)`.

The accepted Aim Test camera and gun animation are now part of **Defense Detonation**, at its existing kit index 8 and default **Shift+2** binding. Existing custom hotbar placement and keys take priority. The separate Aim Test entry is retired; index 10 remains unavailable and hidden without changing shared saved assignments or other classes. Use Defense Detonation again to toggle out: **right-click and Escape do not exit aiming**. The instruction overlay stays removed. Hotbar clicking works when the cursor is available; while mouse-look is captured, the assigned hotbar key toggles the same ability. Other combat abilities remain available and leave aiming when a cast begins.

There is no cast time or channel. Aiming remains available at any stack count and toggling it never spends stacks. **Left-click while the crosshair is visible to fire all available stacks (1–3)**, with a 0.13s interval and fresh aim for each shot. Firing commits the stacks together and applies the ordinary global cooldown. Each shot deals 10% maximum target health on a confirmed body hit, rounded to whole damage with normal mitigation; head hits have no multiplier. Misses consume their shot. Aiming automatically exits after the final authoritative shot result. Right-click ownership is preserved through this exit. The hotbar shows 0–3 uncommitted stacks; newly earned stacks during a burst remain for the next burst.

Holding right-click before entering Defense Detonation preserves that gesture through entry, aiming and exit: normal camera/character turning resumes without releasing and pressing again. Right-button releases during aiming are tracked so exiting cannot leave a stuck hold. A right press begun in aiming can also continue on exit. Casts and movement abilities can take over while retaining the hold; focus loss, menus, death and reset discard it. Only presses owned by gameplay count, so a physically held button from a UI interaction never becomes a synthetic camera gesture.

The camera eases from its current distance to 1.65 m behind Outlaw, offset 0.55 m to his right, with a 62-degree field of view. Entry and exit use the same critically damped spring response as ordinary wheel zoom (24 per second). A single eased blend drives camera distance, shoulder offset, pitch, FOV and gun raise. Velocity is preserved across rapid toggles so reversing direction first brakes the existing motion. The transition completes approximately 99% of its travel in 0.28 s, then settles its tiny remainder. The four short reticle strokes use the exact viewport center and now appear much earlier: at 25% of the shoulder blend, after a camera physics update has checked clearance, instead of waiting for 99.5%. The focused 60 FPS test measures first visibility at about 0.067s. Camera and gun smoothing are unchanged; the crosshair remains visible as they finish moving and hides immediately on exit or loss of aiming control. The normal distance, shoulder offset, pitch, FOV and spring-arm shape are restored on exit; normal distance is bounded to 3–9 m.

Outlaw raises his existing native GunAim pose during the same transition. An upper-arm correction points the held pistol along the camera aim direction while retaining the existing elbow/finger grip. Aim has a ±1-radian pitch limit. Each fired shot adds about 0.25 degrees of upward camera recoil, with a small capped accumulation and smooth recovery; normal mouse input can compensate. The held gun uses its existing native firing motion. Horizontal character facing follows the camera with brief easing, and the usual turning keys strafe during mouse aim. Running speed, reversed walking for backpedal, jump anatomy, clips and source GLB are unchanged. Casting, Roll, Backflip, stun, death, menus, editing, focus loss and round teardown cancel remaining burst shots. Already committed stacks are not refunded. Ordinary exit is blended; loss of the local actor/control context restores the camera immediately.

`scripts/outlaw_aim_test.gd` retains its legacy filename and owns Defense Detonation's local UI/input/camera state. The presenter uses the existing opt-in local arm layer in `scripts/outlaw_art.gd`; `movement_controls.gd` temporarily yields the camera boom and facing controls. A small sphere sweep limits the lateral shoulder offset near terrain, and the existing spring arm limits rearward travel. Detonation's kit entry is marked `local_only`: hotbar dispatch toggles the camera before combat input or RPC dispatch, and the server rejects old targeted Detonation commands. Aiming needs no selected target, cooldown, GCD or spell-school availability. Its existing Detonation icon turns cyan while active. The cosmetic arm state is not replicated yet. Older ten-slot snapshots safely fill only the retired trailing local slot without mutating the received packet; this does not make mixed combat versions interchangeable.

Ordinary wheel zoom and aiming share the same acceleration profile but maintain separate velocity state. Wheel velocity is cleared when Detonation takes the camera; wheel inputs are ignored until the camera returns. The saved preferred distance is retained within the nine-metre limit. See [CAMERA_ZOOM.md](CAMERA_ZOOM.md) for normal zoom tuning.

## Camera-ray burst contract

`Outlaw.detonation_burst_plan(actor)` snapshots the current 1–3 stacks. A full burst schedules at 0, 0.13 and 0.26 seconds; actual server execution is quantized to physics ticks (about 0.133s between shots at 60Hz) and never compresses shots into a faster interval after packet jitter. There is no windup, cast bar or movement restriction. Only one bounded reservation per actor exists, with at most three queued requests and a 1.25s timeout.

`scripts/outlaw_detonation.gd` validates the initial request before calling `Outlaw.reserve_detonation_burst`: peer ownership, class, epoch, action sequence, motion revision, finite normalized aim, bounded timestamp, cooldown/control state and available stacks. The client never supplies damage, a victim or a resource count. Subsequent requests must belong to the same burst, arrive in order and use fresh timestamps. Each execution checks current control state, revision and permission again. Manual exit and cancellation use the same reliable transport channel/delay as the shots. Normal target-based casting remains rejected; this ability cannot silently fall back to a selected target.

The invisible shot is an instantaneous 18m ray from `camera.project_ray_origin()` through `camera.project_ray_normal()` at the viewport center. It is not re-aimed from the chest or gun. The server bounds the origin to the actual shoulder/zoom envelope around a sampled/current player position and checks the pivot-to-camera terrain path. The existing bounded hitbox history resolves nearest body/terrain intersections with up to 250ms rewind; allies and protected world bystanders block without taking damage. Teleports and new lives cannot reuse old target history. Camera and body range are not expanded by weapon length.

The visual endpoint is predicted locally from the displayed bodies/terrain so the gun responds immediately. A separate, reused muzzle-flash/tracer pool emits from the actual held gun, and authoritative results drive remote effects and hit confirmation. These effects perform no collision or damage. A missed ray still produces the muzzle effect. Local predicted effects are not repeated when confirmation arrives. The dedicated server creates no visual model, muzzle pool or textures. Bots retain their existing kit behavior; this pass adds player aimed firing.

The experiment has **not** been added to Model Forge or other classes. The user will review it before deciding whether to make it a shared character feature. Both existing Forge packages remain unchanged and verifiable.

## Validation and review

World shot-timing repair: shot stamps describe a server snapshot received by the client, so their age at the server includes both outbound snapshot travel and inbound shot travel. The allowance now uses the server-measured full RTT, plus the existing interpolation/jitter allowance, still capped at 250ms. The old half-RTT calculation reproduced `Shot timing expired` with 138ms of real UDP relay delay, leaving three unspent stacks behind repeated predicted muzzle effects. World hits and misses now complete and spend the burst at that delay; re-entering aim at zero stacks cannot fire. The 60ms control also passes. Training dummies use negative actor IDs, which now resolve as valid damage targets instead of being discarded by a nonnegative-ID check. World bystanders remain protected.

The network regression delays ENet packets and ACKs through a localhost relay, exercising the actual measured RTT rather than only the existing application-level latency setting. Both world-hit and world-miss cases run in CI. Scoped originals, before/after logs and rollback hashes are under `artifacts/outlaw-detonation-world-timing/`.
