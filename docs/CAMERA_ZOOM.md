# Smooth wheel camera zoom

Updated September 10, 2026. Normal third-person zoom accelerates briefly and decelerates to its preferred distance. Further wheel inputs retain the existing velocity instead of restarting an animation or jumping the camera. The zoom range is now **3–9 metres**: the maximum distance was reduced by 50% from 18 metres. Startup uses the new limit, and older saved preferences above it clamp to 9 metres. Saved fractional distances within the range still work.

`scripts/movement_controls.gd` owns the target and transient velocity. Wheel events route through `scroll_zoom(steps)` with their fractional `InputEventMouseButton.factor`. Each full step multiplies the destination by `exp(-0.08 * steps)`: approximately 7.7% closer or 8.3% farther. Equal opposite inputs are reciprocal away from the limits; four quarter-step events equal one full step. These are selected tuning values, not literal interpretations of the user's illustrative “.01 to 1” example.

The camera uses the exact critically damped spring solution with response 24 per second. It begins moving immediately, reaches maximum speed after roughly 42 ms from rest, then settles to within 1% of a single step in approximately 0.28 s. Position and velocity stay continuous when the requested distance changes, including reversals. Submillimetre residual motion is stopped once the velocity is negligible. Updates are independent of frame rate, and SpringArm3D continues to limit actual camera placement against terrain.

Menus and loss of camera focus suspend zoom and reject world scrolling. Reloading preferences or cancelling controls clears transient velocity. Outlaw's Aim Test now uses the same spring response for entry and exit, with one eased blend driving the camera and gun raise together. It takes exclusive ownership of the camera during those transitions; ordinary wheel inertia cannot disturb them or change the preferred distance.

Validation: `tests/camera_zoom_test.gd` passes 33 checks on Godot 4.7.2 and 4.5.1, covering proportional/fractional input, acceleration, coast and settling, reversal, 30/60/144 FPS timing, the halved distance limit, menus/focus, saved settings and shoulder-camera isolation. It runs in CI through `tests/check_suite.sh "Camera zoom checks"`. A rendered second-monitor review also recorded individual camera distances and velocities in `artifacts/aim-smoothing-range/review/zoom-frames.csv`; these are animation samples, not a performance benchmark. Scoped backups, logs and installation hashes are alongside that review. The preceding wheel/hotbar update remains separately backed up under `artifacts/camera-zoom-hotbar/`.
